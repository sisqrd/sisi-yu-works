E-textiles · Physical fabrication

Cyclist safety jacket

Drivers can't read a cyclist's hand signals at night. This jacket puts the turn signal on the rider's back: a lit arrow points left or right, so the car behind knows where the bike is going.

I sketched it, wired it and sewed it into an everyday quilted jacket, then tested it on a mannequin and on a rider.

  • 01Addressable LED strips shaped into left and right arrows
  • 02Translucent back panel with sewn-in fabric pockets for the parts
  • 03Arduino Uno and breadboard hidden in the lining
  • 04Concept: infrared proximity sensor to detect cars approaching from behind
Morphing Matter Lab · CMU · 2019

Actuating Skin

Soft, programmable surfaces that change shape on command. We cast silicone modules with air channels built in, so when air flows in, the surface swells, bulges and folds like a living skin.

The modules tile into larger patterns, such as an octagonal panel or a hinged strip, so a flat sheet can turn into a 3D form.

  • 01Laser-cut acrylic molds with channel patterns
  • 02Multi-layer cast silicone with embedded air chambers
  • 03Pump, valves and microcontroller with "extrude" and "stop" controls
  • 04Tests of inflation sequences, folding pairs and touch-triggered shapes

Morphing Matter Lab, led by Prof. Lining Yao (CMU HCII)
With Fang Qin, Jay Huh, Katherine Giesa and Maria Vlachostergiou

A-301AI trust, ethics & creativity

What I want AI to be

I've been working on this since my time at Carnegie Mellon, well before AI design was a job title. Two questions drive me.

01 · Trust & ethics

Can people trust what the machine sees?

Models learn from datasets, and datasets carry the biases of whoever collected and labeled them. I design ways for people to see how AI reads them, to correct it, and to decide whether their data is used at all.

  • Thesis on curating image datasets to reduce harmful bias
  • JIJO: consent-first, crowdsourced dataset collection
  • Research on ethical AI systems at CMU
02 · Creativity

Can AI make people more creative?

Generative models open up new creative freedom, as long as the tools give artists real control. I prototype interfaces where AI is a collaborator rather than a vending machine.

  • Adobe: AI-assisted feedback for next-gen creative tools
  • CMU Creative AI: GAN and text-to-image concept studies
  • Now: prototyping with AI as a working designer at Stripe

CMU Creative AI · 2019

JIJOformerly DoppleGANer · a crowdsourced image dataset tool

Take a selfie and label yourself: gender, ethnicity, age, mood, who you are. Then see how a commercial face recognition API labels the same photo, side by side. The gap is the lesson.

FullUse my photo and labels in the dataset.
PartialUse my labels with a GAN-generated face that resembles me.
NoneDestroy my image. Nothing is stored.

The goal is a slow-growing, self-labeled dataset collected transparently, as an alternative to scraping faces without consent.

Master's thesis · Carnegie Mellon · 2019 · Advised by Eunsu Kang & Jean Oh

Who Is the Fairest of Them All? Image dataset curation to mitigate harmful bias for machine learning applications

Data curation covers how a dataset is collected, labeled and reshaped. Common practice leans on large, supposedly generic datasets, which is how social biases end up in systems used in education, employment and criminal justice. When the ACLU tested Amazon's Rekognition, it falsely matched 28 members of Congress with arrest photos, disproportionately people of color. Most fixes treat this as a problem with the algorithm, not with the people and data behind it.

The thesis asks four questions: what harm biased datasets cause, why the bias exists, what current practice does about it, and what a human-centered alternative looks like. Along the way it shows how recognition tools label weddings around the world and how datasets picture something as ordinary as a toilet depending on a family's income.

It closes with a design framework for curating data instead of scraping it, built as the JIJO prototype above: people label themselves, see how AI labels them, and choose whether to contribute.
